Excerpts and simplifications. GOV.UK page retrieved 2026-10-27. The capital limit and taper come from the national pensioner scheme in SI 2012/2885 (summarised, not quoted). Each council runs its own scheme, so this is a simplified pensioner model.

[CTR-1] "You could be eligible if you're on a low income or claim benefits. Your bill could be reduced by up to 100%." Each council runs its own scheme. This model covers pensioners in England and Wales. (source: https://www.gov.uk/council-tax-reduction)

[CTR-2] The pensioner scheme applies to people who have reached State Pension age. (source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/2885/contents)

[CTR-3] The applicant must be liable to pay council tax on the home they live in. (source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/2885/contents)

[CTR-4] People who get the Guarantee Credit of Pension Credit get the maximum reduction (100% of the net council tax charge). (source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/2885/contents)

[CTR-5] Otherwise, people with capital above £16,000 cannot get a reduction. (source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/2885/contents)

[CTR-6] Otherwise, the weekly reduction is the weekly net council tax charge (annual net charge divided by 52), less 20% of any weekly income above the applicable amount. Simplification: the applicable amount is the Pension Credit guarantee amount (£238 single, £363.25 couple), and income counted from savings follows the Pension Credit rule (£1 a week for every £500, or part of £500, over £10,000). The household is eligible if the weekly reduction is greater than zero. (source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/2885/contents)
